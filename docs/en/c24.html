20Equipment & network

Everything to prepare before installing BrewDesk in a store — devices, printers, card readers and the network — in one place. It gathers tips spread across the device modes, device pairing, offline and printer chapters, in installation order. For the actual install sequence, follow Installation checklist.

Devices

You can use tablets and computers you already own. However, connecting devices to each other (registering staff ordering, customer display and waitlist kiosk devices) only works in the installed BrewDesk app.

DeviceHow it's used · notes
iPadInstall the BrewDesk app. It can take any job: main till, extra till, staff ordering, customer display or waitlist kiosk. It prints straight from the app to Wi-Fi/LAN printers or AirPrint printers (Receipts & printers).
Android tabletNot supported yet (the Android app is in preparation). Use an iPad or the desktop app for now.
Computer (Mac · Windows)The BrewDesk desktop app is recommended. USB printers are connected through the desktop app's "Select installed printer", so a till with a USB printer needs the desktop app (Receipts & printers).
Web browserYou can sign up, set up and even run a till at brewdesk.io. But a browser screen can't be registered as a new device ("Add devices from the BrewDesk app"), and Wi-Fi printing from a browser may ask you to install a helper program (Print Bridge). Install the app on any device that stays in the store.
How many devices? It depends on the plan — tills (including the main one): Starter and Pro 1, Business 3. Customer displays: Pro 1, Business 3. Staff ordering devices: Pro 3, Business unlimited. Extra tills (Sub POS) are a Business feature (Device modes & customer display, device limit).
Make the main till a device that stays on all day. Orders from staff-ordering tablets and QR / online orders all pass through the main till. If it goes off, QR / online ordering pauses, and nothing switches to another device automatically. To connect other devices, the main till must also be running the BrewDesk app (Multiple devices & reliability).

Receipt & kitchen printers

  1. Type — any standard thermal receipt printer. The defaults suit 80mm paper (42 characters per line).
  2. Wi-Fi / LAN printers — connect with the printer's IP address and port (usually 9100). If you don't know the IP, tap "Search". Put the printer on the same router as the tills.
  3. USB printers — install the printer driver on the computer as usual (Mac is usually automatic, Windows uses the maker's driver), then pick it from the list in the desktop app.
  4. Role — give each printer a role: Main Receipt (customer receipts) or Kitchen (kitchen tickets). One printer can do both, and selling works fine with no kitchen printer at all.
  5. Cash drawer — the drawer opens through the receipt printer, and its cash-drawer option must be on in the printer settings. The drawer does not open from an extra till (Sub POS) (Cash drawer).
Check with a test print. Use "Test Print" to confirm paper actually comes out and text isn't cut off or garbled. Some Wi-Fi printers can't report whether they printed ("No print confirmation"), so check with your eyes (Receipts & printers).

Card readers

  1. Connected card readers — BrewDesk uses Stripe Terminal readers. Money goes straight to the owner's own Stripe account. Readers are bought from the Stripe Dashboard.
  2. Connecting — first connect the owner's Stripe account in Settings › Payments. Power on the reader, put it on the same network as the till, then in the card reader section of the same screen tap "Discover Readers" → "Connect".
  3. An existing separate terminal — stores that take cards on a terminal not connected to BrewDesk record them with the card-brand buttons on the payment screen (Record Only). Make sure Card (Record Only) is on in Settings › Payments (Getting paid).
Connected card payments need the internet. When it drops you'll need another way to get paid, so we recommend leaving the Cash payment method on (What needs the internet).

Wi-Fi & network

BrewDesk devices talk to the main till over the store's own network, even when the internet is down. That makes the router the most important part of the install.

  1. Everything on the same store Wi-Fi (same router) — the main till, extra tills, staff-ordering tablets, customer displays, network printers and card readers all go on one network. Different Wi-Fi networks are the most common reason devices can't find each other.
  2. No guest Wi-Fi, no phone hotspots — if the store runs a separate guest Wi-Fi, keep store devices on the store Wi-Fi only. A device on guest Wi-Fi is told "This device is outside the store network".
  3. Turn off AP isolation — if the router has "AP isolation" or "wireless isolation" switched on, devices can't talk to each other even on the same Wi-Fi. Turn it off for the store Wi-Fi.
  4. Give printers a static IP — if a Wi-Fi/LAN printer's IP address changes, BrewDesk keeps printing to the old one. Setting a static IP on the printer or router avoids that.
  5. Changed the router? — devices may no longer find the main till. Pair them again from the main till and re-check the printer IPs (Add a device to your main till, checking the link).

Ready for internet outages

  1. Selling carries on — orders, cash payments and receipt printing keep working offline. Everything uploads by itself when the internet returns, never twice (Working offline).
  2. What truly needs the internet — connected card payments, sending messages, and creating or changing bookings (What needs the internet).
  3. Keep the router powered — as long as the store Wi-Fi is up, staff-ordering tablets and extra tills keep talking to the main till, and you can even pair new devices (Pairing devices offline).
  4. Sign in once on every device on install day — offline, only an account that has signed in on that device before can get in (Signing in offline).
