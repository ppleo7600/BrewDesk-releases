3Setting up your store

Now let's fill in the basics about your shop. You only do this once. Take it slow — one box at a time.

  1. Tap Settings (the gear icon) near the bottom of the left sidebar.
  2. Tap Store Info.
  3. Type your shop's name, address, and the money you use (like dollars or pounds).
  4. Check your business type is right (cafe, restaurant, and so on). This sets up tables, staff, and PIN logins for you.
  5. Open Hours & Closures and type the times your shop is open. These times tell BrewDesk when people can book a table, join the line, and order by phone.
  6. Open the tax setting. BrewDesk adds the right tax for you, so you don't have to do the math.
  7. Add your receipt bits, like your logo and a thank-you message at the bottom.
  8. Tap Save. Your shop is now set up.
Tip: "Tax" is the extra bit of money the government asks for on a sale. BrewDesk works it out for you — you just turn it on.
Time zone is worth a second look. If the device you're on is in a different time zone from your store setting, a banner appears at the top: "This device's time zone (…) is different from your store's setting (…). Sales days and closing reports follow the store setting, so please make sure it is correct." Tap "Check time zone" to jump straight to the setting. It only appears when the clocks really differ, so it won't nag you for two cities that share a time. On your main till the wording is stronger — that's the device your business day is measured from.

Good to know: some countries have e-invoicing or fiscal-record rules we don't support yet, so they aren't offered. If you pick one and save, you'll see "This country is not supported." If your country isn't on the list, get in touch with support.

Set your tax

Tax is the extra bit of money the government asks for on a sale. You only set this up once, and then BrewDesk adds it for you on every sale. Let's get your rate right.

Where to find it

Tap Settings in the left sidebar, then open Settings › Tax & Service.

  1. BrewDesk can suggest a rate for your area, but you confirm it. Tap "Refresh Now" to fetch a suggested rate, or just type your own.
  2. Type your rate in the "Tax" box on the Taxes card (it has a % next to it) — for example, type 8 for 8%.
  3. Choose how tax shows. "Tax Inclusive Pricing" ON means tax is already inside the price you show. OFF means tax is added on top at checkout.
  4. Optional: add a "Service Charge" (a % or a fixed amount), and choose whether the service charge is taxed.
  5. Tap "Save". A live receipt preview on the right shows a sample calculation, so you can check it looks right.
  6. A note reminds you that tax rates are suggestions — confirm yours with your tax advisor.
Not sure which rate to pick? Don't worry. Tap "Refresh Now" for a suggestion to start with, then check it with your tax advisor or accountant. You can change it any time.
Tax-inclusive store? Receipts now label the tax line "Tax (incl.)" when "Tax Inclusive Pricing" is ON — so customers can see the tax is already built into the price, not added on top.
"Tax Amount Rounding" — renamed, and now it says what it does. The setting that used to be called "Tax Rounding Rule" is now "Tax Amount Rounding": "Sets how the tax amount is reduced to the currency's smallest unit — cents in most currencies, whole yen or won where the currency has no decimals. Applies to every order, whatever the customer pays with." The choices are the same four — "Standard (0.005 → Round Up)", "Always Round Up", "Always Round Down", "No Rounding". Underneath, BrewDesk now shows the usual rule for your country as a reference: "Country default for … : … . Your setting is what the POS uses." — a reference only; your choice wins. Two small chips next to it say where you stand at a glance: "prices include tax" or "tax added at checkout", and "service charge is taxed" or "service charge is not taxed". Nothing about your existing tax numbers changed with this rename.
Don't mix this up with cash rounding. "Tax Amount Rounding" is about the tax figure and applies to every sale. Matching the final total to a coin you actually have — the 1-cent piece nobody carries — is a separate setting under Settings › Payments, and it only ever touches cash. See 7.3.

Different items, different tax? Use "Tax types"

Some places tax things differently — for example, plain groceries may have no tax while a hot drink does. If that's you, BrewDesk can keep a few tax types and you pick the right one for each item. If every item in your shop uses the same rate, you can skip this — nothing changes for you.

Where to find it

Open Settings › Tax & Service and look for the "Tax types" card. It shows up for shops in places that need it (Canada, for example), and for some US shops that turn it on.

  1. Tap "Set up tax types". BrewDesk fills in the usual taxes for your region. If yours look different, a yellow note says "These taxes don't match the standard for your region." — tap "Use regional standard" to fix them in one tap.
  2. Under "Taxes", each tax has a "Tax name" and a "Tax rate (%)". Tap "Add tax" to add another one. This is the only place a tax rate is set.
  3. Under "Tax types", tap "Add tax type", give it a "Tax type name", and tick the "Taxes that apply". Choose "No tax (exempt)" for things that carry no tax at all.
  4. One type is marked "Default". It's used for any item that has no tax type of its own, and for the service charge.
  5. Go to Manage › Menu Items in the left sidebar. On a category, pick "Tax type for this category". On an item, pick "Tax type". Leave an item on "Follow category" unless it is taxed differently.
BrewDesk may suggest one for you. If an item looks like the others in its category, you'll see "Suggested: … — not confirmed". Nothing changes until you tap "Use this".
Lower tax on small amounts. Some places charge less tax when the price is small — Ontario, for example, rebates part of the tax on prepared food and drinks of $4 or less. Turn on "Use an amount condition" under "Lower tax below an amount", pick the two taxes it switches between, and choose whether to measure the "Order total" or "Each item's unit price". A short sentence below it tells you in plain words what the rule will do.

Good to know: receipts and reports show each tax on its own line, so two taxes can't share the same name — BrewDesk asks you to rename one. A few places (Massachusetts and Rhode Island) tax only the part above a limit; the amount condition can't copy that, so a warning appears there. Always check your setup with your tax advisor.

Holidays & special days

Mark the days you're closed, and BrewDesk stops customers from booking a table, joining the queue, or ordering from their phone on that day. Public holidays, staff training, a sudden day off — you set them all here.

Where to find it

Open Settings › Hours & Closures and look for "Holidays & Special Days" lower down the page. You can use it even without the Reservations add-on turned on. The calendar sits on the left; the list of closures for the month you're viewing sits on the right.

  1. Tap "+ Add" above the list, tap a date on the calendar, or tap an existing entry in the list — a pop-up opens.
  2. Pick the date(s). Choose "One day" for a single date, or "Date range" to close a stretch of days at once (up to 31) — handy for multi-day holidays.
  3. Turn on "Closed all day". Once it's on, choose what stops under "Services to close" — reservations, waitlist, online & QR ordering. You can close just one and leave the rest open.
  4. Type a short "Label" like "Christmas" or "Staff training". Whatever you write in "Message shown to guests" is what your customers will see.
  5. Tap Save. It shows up on the calendar and in the list right away.
Leaving "Closed all day" off blocks saving. Right now you can only register a closure — custom opening hours for a single day are marked "Coming soon" and can't be saved yet. The old "Limited Capacity" option (per-day capacity limits) is also gone; use the service checkboxes in step 3 instead if you only want to close some services.
Already have bookings that day? When you save a single day, a pop-up lets you choose one of three things — "Leave as is", "Notify only" (keep the bookings, just send a closure notice), or "Cancel all" (cancel every booking and tell the guests). Saving a closure never deletes bookings on its own; it only does what you pick. For a date range, this pop-up doesn't appear — after saving, open each day from the list to notify or cancel.
You can still fix last month's entries. Both the calendar and the list can move to previous months, so you can edit or delete an old closure. To remove one, tap the delete icon on its row in the list.
A weekday you close every week isn't set here — just switch that day off in the business days row at the top of the same screen, and it repeats automatically. Those show up in the list with a separate "Regular" badge.
"Repeat every year" repeats on the solar calendar date. Lunar holidays (like Lunar New Year) land on a different date each year, so it's safer to mark those by hand every year. Also note: a yearly repeat does block bookings on that date, but the guest booking calendar doesn't grey it out yet — guests only see the closure notice after they pick that date.
Show this off

Mark the day off once, and everything closes together. Your booking page, your queue and your customers' phone ordering all shut for that day — no running around switching each one off, and multi-day holidays can be registered in one go.

See your service status at a glance

Can customers book right now? Join the queue? Order from their phone? One set of buttons tells you straight away — so you know before a customer tells you something isn't working.

Where to find it

Tap Tables in the left sidebar to open the Table Map screen — the toolbar at the top has three buttons: Online Orders, Reservations and Waitlist. Nothing to switch on — they're always there. (These used to be small badges in the top bar; they now live as bigger buttons on the Table Map.)

  1. The button colour tells you the state — coloured means it's running normally, grey means you've turned it off, grey with a clock means it's paused for now, and grey with a lock means it's blocked by something like your plan or payment setup.
  2. Tap a button to open "What customers can use right now".
  3. It tells you why something is closed — for example "Closed today", "Outside business hours", "Main POS is offline", "Requires the {plan} plan", "Payment setup required", or "Monthly order limit reached".
  4. Need to stop taking something for a bit? Tap "Pause for today…" at the bottom.
  5. Pick the service and how long — 20 minutes, 60 minutes, Until close today (it reopens by itself at your next opening), or Until I turn it back on.
You can also pause and resume from the side panel. Open the Online Orders, Reservations, or Waitlist side panel and there's a "Pause"/"Resume" button at the bottom — it opens the same window as the Table Map buttons.
Perfect when the kitchen is slammed. Pause online orders for "20 minutes" and keep serving the people in front of you while you catch up. It reopens on its own.
Who can change it. Only the owner and managers can pause a service — anyone else gets a "you don't have permission" note. Everyone can still see the status.
If the Reservations add-on is off, the Reservations button won't show at all — turn the add-on back on to see it again.

Good to know: the reason wording also shows on your customers' screen, so they aren't left wondering why they can't order.
