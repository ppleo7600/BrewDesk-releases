16Staff & permissions

Here you add the people who work for you. You can pick what each one is allowed to do, and track their work hours. (This needs the Pro or Business plan.)

Where to turn it on

Turn it on in Settings › Add-ons › Staff (a Pro add-on; only the owner can turn it on).

  1. Go to Settings and find the staff page.
  2. Tap Add staff. Type the person's name.
  3. Give them a role. The role decides what they can and can't do in the app.
  4. Turn on Idle Lock if you want the till to lock itself when nobody's using it. (This used to be called "PIN Login".)
  5. Turn on the time clock so staff can tap to start and end their shift. Their hours go into your reports for you.
  6. You can put a staff member's name on an order any time — even after the sale is done. (This is handy for salons.)
A role is the set of things a person is allowed to do. For example, only a manager might be allowed to give refunds.

Staff add-on on vs off

Staff features are fully on only when both are true: (1) your plan is Pro or Business, and (2) the Staff toggle in Settings › Add-ons is on. If either is missing, parts are locked as shown below.

  • ON: the Staff Sales and Attendance Log tabs in Stats open (the Stats screen starts on Revenue Overview). Payment History gets an All Staff filter and the Close Day window shows staff chips. With Idle Lock also on, the register shows a Select Profile lock screen so each person switches to their own profile.
  • OFF: those tabs show a lock, and the All Staff filter, Close Day staff chips and lock screen do not appear. Adding and viewing people in Settings › Staff Permissions › Staff still works on Pro, whatever the toggle.
  • Starter plan: the Add-ons card shows an Upgrade button instead of a toggle. The Staff sub-tab shows only a lock, "Staff management is Pro-only" and an Upgrade Pro button.
Relation to the Scheduler: Scheduler Staff Columns depend on the Pro plan plus having staff accounts, not on this toggle (see chapter 6). Starter has no Scheduler tab at all.

Add a staff member

  1. Tap "Add staff". A form opens.
  2. Type the person's Name.
  3. Pick a Role — "Manager" (menu, orders, reports — no billing or staff changes) or "Cashier" (take orders, see today's sales).
  4. Set a 4-digit PIN and confirm it.
  5. Tap "Add staff". They now appear in your staff list and can sign in with their PIN.

Edit or remove a person

Fixing a name, changing a PIN, hiding someone who left — these used to be four different little controls on the same row. Now there is one door: tap the person.

  1. In your staff list, tap anywhere on that person's row. Their card opens.
  2. Their email is at the top so you can be sure it's the right person. Fix the Name if you need to.
  3. Change the Role if their job changed. (An owner's row shows the role as plain text — an owner's role can't be changed here.)
  4. To set a new PIN, type it in both PIN (4 digits) boxes. Leave both boxes blank and the current PIN stays as it is — the screen says so, right under the boxes.
  5. Tap "Save". That's it.
Half-typed PINs don't slip through. If you type even one digit, BrewDesk asks for all four and asks you to type it twice. A PIN that's quietly ignored is worse than no PIN.
Hide someone instead of deleting them. "Disable" takes them off the POS but keeps their past orders and hours in your reports. "Enable" brings them back. Only the owner sees these buttons.
Invited the wrong person as an owner? You can now remove an owner too — before, there was no way to undo that. Two things still can't happen, and BrewDesk tells you which: you can't remove yourself, and you can't remove the last owner of the shop. Removing anyone asks you to confirm the name, then asks for the owner PIN ("Owner PIN required to remove staff member.").

Removing someone is not the same as handing the shop over. If you're selling the business, ask us about transferring ownership instead.

Permissions, manager PIN & role chip

This settings page shows, screen by screen, what a manager and a cashier are each allowed to do — and now you can actually tick the boxes and save. The whole page was rebuilt: your two PIN cards sit side by side across the top, with the big table below.

Where to find it

Settings › Staff Permissions. Managers can open it and read it; only the owner can change and save it. Two tabs sit at the top — "Permissions" (everyone gets this) and "Staff" (a Pro feature: "Staff management is Pro-only").

  1. The table has exactly two columns — "Manager" and "Staff". There's no owner column, because "The owner always has every permission".
  2. Permissions are grouped by screen. Tap a group to unfold it, or tap "Expand all" to open everything at once.
  3. Tick or untick a box for that role. A group header tells you where it stands at a glance: "All on", "Partly on" or "Off".
  4. Watch the coloured pills next to some rows: "PIN approval" (a manager has to type a PIN first), "Limited view" (they see part of it), "Restricted" (blocked), and "Open the page" — the one that decides whether the screen appears at all.
  5. Can't find something in a long list? Use "Search permissions", or flip on "Differences only" to show just the rows where manager and staff differ.
  6. Tap Save at the top of Settings when you're done. Unsaved rows are counted for you, and "Discard changes" puts everything back.
Every row you see in the table is really enforced. Untick a box and that role is actually stopped, with a popup saying so. The table currently has refunds, managing discounts, recalculating a closing & settling tips, adding staff & resetting their PINs and saving settings, and opening a screen at all ("Open the page") is enforced too — see the next two tips.
Managers can no longer save settings by default. This one changed: "saving settings" now starts off for managers. A manager can still open a settings screen and read it, but tapping Save brings up "You don't have permission for this" — "Only the store owner can make this change. You can still view this screen." If your manager used to save settings for you, tick that box here to give it back.
You can hand a manager whole settings tabs. Eleven settings tabs can be opened up to a manager — Hours & Closures, Receipts, Printers, Devices, Staff Permissions (read-only), Activity Log, Support, Tables, Waitlist, Reservations and Staff. Fourteen stay owner-only no matter what, because they're the money and the identity of the shop: Store Info, Tax & Service, Payments, Notifications, Add-ons, Tips, Order Types, Customers, Customer Portal, Scheduler, Display & Branding, Loyalty, Google Drive and Messaging. Those don't have a box to tick.
When someone hits a screen you switched off they get told plainly, in place of the screen: "The store owner turned this screen off for your role" — "Access to this screen was switched off for your role under Settings › Staff Permissions. Signing in again won't restore it — ask the store owner to turn it back on." That last sentence matters: this is a setting, not a glitch, so logging out and back in won't help them.
Every save emails you. Whenever permissions are saved, the store owner gets an email saying what changed — so nobody can quietly widen their own access. The same change only emails once a day.
Idle Lock off doesn't switch any of this off. Turning Idle Lock off (under Settings › Staff Permissions › Staff) only stops the till locking itself — nothing more. Staff still sign in with their own PIN, the role chip still switches, the activity log still records who did what, and every permission on this page is still enforced. (Turning the Staff add-on off is the switch that stops people being told apart.)

The PIN cards — side by side across the top of this page

The owner PIN and manager PIN cards live on this same page, side by side across the top. Each one tells you its state at a glance: "Set", "Default 0000" or "Not set". Under the owner card: "Locks after 10 min idle" once it's set, or "Set it to enable the lock screen" if it isn't. The manager PIN lets whoever is on shift do manager things without a separate account for them — only the owner can set or change it. A manager opening this page sees a padlock and "Only the owner can change this card."

Still on 0000? Then Manager is shut. The card says so — status chip "Default 0000" and, underneath, "Can't switch to Manager until you change it". While the manager PIN is the factory 0000, nobody can switch this till to Manager at all: typing 0000 gets "This PIN is still the default, so it can't be used to switch roles. Set a new PIN first." Change it once and Manager works. (This is on purpose — a shop-wide code everyone already knows isn't a lock.)
Worth knowing before you rely on it: your activity log will say "Manager", not who. If you need to know which person did something, give people their own staff accounts (Pro).

The role chip — switching who this till is right now

  1. Look at the top bar: there's a coloured chip showing what this POS is acting as — Owner (amber), Manager (blue) or Staff (grey).
  2. Tap it. A small panel opens with the three roles listed.
  3. Stepping down (owner → staff, say, when you hand the till to someone) is one tap. No PIN — you're giving power away, not taking it.
  4. Stepping up asks for that role's PIN ("Enter the manager PIN"). Rows this device isn't allowed to reach are greyed out before you tap, so you never hit a dead end.
BrewDesk won't let you lock yourself out on the way down. If there's no working PIN to climb back up with, stepping down is refused before it happens: "Set the owner PIN first. Without it, this device could not switch back up after stepping down." Set your owner PIN and it's one tap again. (A PIN still sitting on the factory 0000 doesn't count as a way back.)
Typed the PIN wrong five times? That one device pauses PIN switching for 15 minutes: "Too many PIN attempts on this device — it's locked for a few minutes. Other devices and personal PIN sign-in are not affected." Read that last part — your other tills carry on, and anyone with their own staff PIN can still sign in normally, so the queue keeps moving. The owner also gets an email about repeated wrong tries, at most one a day.

Forgot a PIN?

Nobody has to phone support because a PIN went out of somebody's head. Each PIN has its own way back.

The owner PIN — a code to your email

Where to find it

Tap the role chip in the top bar (the same panel you use to switch role). On the owner row there's "Forgot your PIN?". You must be signed in as the owner on this device.

  1. Tap "Forgot your PIN?". BrewDesk emails a 6-digit code to the account email: "We sent a 6-digit code to your account email. Enter it below along with a new PIN."
  2. Type the Email code, then your New PIN (4 digits), and confirm it.
  3. Tap "Set new PIN". You'll see "Owner PIN reset. Use the new PIN next time."
The code doesn't hang around. It expires after 10 minutes, and 5 wrong tries lock it — you'll see "Too many incorrect attempts. Request a new code." Just send yourself a fresh one. Too simple a PIN is refused too: "That PIN is too simple — choose a different 4 digits."
You get an email even when it was you. Every owner PIN reset sends a confirmation to the owner's inbox. If one ever arrives and it wasn't you, that's your early warning.

The manager PIN — the owner just sets a new one

  1. Open Settings › Staff Permissions and find the manager PIN card across the top.
  2. Tap "Change PIN" (or "Set PIN" if there isn't one yet) and type the new code. Owner only.
  3. Tell the counter the new code.
The "Reset to 0000" button is gone from the manager PIN. It used to be there; it isn't any more, and there's nothing to miss — resetting to 0000 only ever left the shop with a code everybody knew, and 0000 can't be used anyway. Just set the new PIN directly. One staff member's PIN is different: Settings › Staff Permissions › Staff, open that person, and "Reset to 0000" is still there for you — an owner or a manager can use it. Have them set their own PIN straight away afterwards.
0000 doesn't work anywhere. Signing in with the default gets "The default PIN (0000) can't be used to sign in. Ask the owner to set a new PIN in Settings, or use ‘Forgot your PIN?’ there." — and now switching role with it is refused too: "This PIN is still the default, so it can't be used to switch roles. Set a new PIN first." A forgotten reset can't quietly become an open door.
