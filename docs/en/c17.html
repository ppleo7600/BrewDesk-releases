17Reports & closing the day

At the end of the day you check how the shop did and "close" the day. This locks in the day's numbers so they are safe. Here's how.

  1. Open Stats (it starts on Revenue Overview) to see your sales, how people paid, your best-selling items, and what each staff member sold.
  2. Read your AI daily insights (Pro and above). This is a short note in plain words about your day, with helpful tips. (Business plans also get one by email each week.)
  3. Count the cash in your drawer and check it matches the number BrewDesk shows. Add any money you put in, and note any money you took out.
  4. When the cash matches, go to Manage › Closing History (the screen is titled Closing Reports), tap Close Now, then tap Close Day in the window that opens. This saves a full summary of the day, called the Z-Report.
What is closing the day? It just means you finish up for the day and save the totals. Doing it each night keeps your money records tidy.
Practice orders are labelled, not hidden. Anything you rang up before going live now carries a "Sandbox data" badge in your payment history and your closing history — "Recorded during practice mode, before this store went live." Your Stats screen goes further and leaves them out of the numbers altogether, so your sales figures and staff sales are only real trading. If your totals look lower than they used to, this is why — they're right now.
Had a partial refund today? The Z-Report shows a "Refunds" line in amber for any partial refunds. That amount is already counted in the Grand Total below it — the line is just there so the numbers tie out cleanly for you or your accountant. In the analytics report you download from Stats (CSV), "Net Profit" is now labeled "Net Revenue (after tax)" — a reminder it's revenue, not profit, since the cost of goods isn't subtracted. The Analytics Excel file in Backup & Restore may still use the older name.
Gave something away today? The closing summary now shows a separate line under Discounts — "FREE ITEMS (3)" with "Not deducted from total" beneath it. Free is not the same as discounted: a discount is money you chose to give up on a sale, a freebie is stock that walked out with nothing coming back. Seeing them apart is how you notice when "just one on the house" becomes twenty a week.
Rounding cash to the nearest coin? If you switched on cash payment rounding (7.3), the Payment Breakdown grows one line — "Cash Rounding", right under Total Payment Collected, with "Coins not taken on cash payments. Payment Collected + this = Grand Total." underneath. That line is why collected and grand total differ, and it's the number to hand your accountant. If rounding is off, or nothing rounded today, the line doesn't appear at all.
Cancelling an order asks why — always. Whether you void a leftover unpaid order at closing, void them all at once, or cancel an order out on the floor, the same little box appears: Wrong entry, Customer left, Test order, or Other (type a reason). The confirm button stays greyed out until you pick one. Cancellations with no reason are exactly the ones you'll want an answer for later.
Show this off

Your numbers, said in plain words. Daily insights tell you what happened and what to do next — not just a page full of figures.

Export your sales

Hand your numbers to your bookkeeper in one tap. Here's how. (The window is titled "Backup & Restore" — the same one used for backups in Chapter 19.)

  1. On the Closing Reports page (Manage › Closing History), tap "Accounting Export". A window titled "Backup & Restore" opens.
  2. Tick what to include: "Closing Report Summary", "Daily Breakdown", "All Transactions", or a ready-made "QuickBooks Format" / "Xero Format".
  3. Tap "Download" to save the file, or "Save to Drive" to send it to your Google Drive. The format depends on what you ticked — Closing Report Summary and Daily Breakdown come as Excel (.xlsx); All Transactions, QuickBooks Format and Xero Format come as CSV.
Save to Drive only works once Google Drive is connected. If the top of the window says "Google Drive not connected", tap Connect next to it (see Chapter 19). Download works either way.
Easy bookkeeping: this makes your records simple — just hand the file to your accountant.

Late orders & missed days

Two things sometimes happen around closing: a payment lands after you've already closed the day, or you simply forgot to close a day. BrewDesk handles both — and never changes your original Z-Report.

A payment came in after closing — Additional Closing

  1. If you take a payment for a day that's already closed, BrewDesk warns you first: "This business day is already closed." The payment still goes through as normal.
  2. A little later a prompt appears: "Additional Closing Needed." Tap "Review & Close" (or "Not Now" to handle it later).
  3. Check the late orders listed, then tap "Confirm Additional Closing." BrewDesk saves a separate report called "Additional Closing #1" — your original Z-Report is never touched.
Need the paper again? Open that day's closing report — each additional closing shows as a badge you can tap to reprint. (Additional closings print totals only, no payment-method or category breakdown.)

You forgot to close a day

  1. The next time you open BrewDesk, an "Unclosed Business Days" window lists any day you missed. New orders and payments pause until those days are closed.
  2. Read "What should I do?" at the top. The easy choice is "Auto-Close All" — it closes every missed day at once using BrewDesk's expected totals (the cash count is skipped).
  3. Prefer to check each one? Tap "Close One by One." If a day still has unpaid orders, tap "Settle" to pay or void them first.
Paying off a bill that's already open still works. Only new business pauses. An order that's already sitting on a table can still be settled — "Pay Now" and "Split" stay available, so you can take the money, clear the order, and then close the day.
The number it shows is the real one. If closing is refused because a bill is still unpaid, the message tells you how many orders the till is actually waiting on, so you know what to go and settle.
Why block new sales? Closing days in order keeps your totals honest, so your reports — and your accountant's numbers — always line up.

Cash drawer

You can balance the cash in your drawer for each shift. Put in the starting amount in the morning, note any money you add or take out, then count it at the end and see if it matches.

Where to find it

The Manage menu at the top → "Cash Drawer." Only the owner and managers see this menu item.

  1. When you open up, tap "Start Session" and enter the starting amount you put in the drawer.
  2. During the day, note money you add or remove as Pay In or Pay Out — bank drops, tip-outs, topping up change, and so on.
  3. At the end, tap "END SESSION" and enter what you actually counted. There are two ways: "Enter Total" types one total, or "Count Denoms" lets you enter how many of each note and coin and adds it up for you.
  4. BrewDesk shows Expected, Counted and Variance side by side. Cash sales are added in and money handed back as "Cash Refunds" is taken out.
  5. Then tap Close Day — the drawer figures go into that day's Z-Report with everything else.
Not counting is recorded as "not counted," not as "zero difference." If you finish without entering anything, BrewDesk doesn't invent a variance of zero — it writes "Cash wasn't counted." Later, when you look back, "it balanced" and "nobody counted" won't look the same.
Drawer not popping open by itself? The screen tells you why — ① no receipt printer is set up yet, ② the cash drawer setting is off on your main receipt printer (turn it on in Settings › Printers), or ③ this device is a Sub POS. Either way, your figures are still recorded.
If unpaid orders are still open, BrewDesk asks once more before it closes the drawer — "N unpaid order(s) still open for today. End the drawer session anyway?" That stops you closing the drawer while a bill is still waiting.
Closing the drawer before the day is fine. Count it, tap END SESSION, then Close Day — what you counted and the variance still show up in the closing report and on the printout.
An open drawer now holds the day open. If a drawer session is still running, the Close Day button is greyed out and a red line appears: "N cash drawer session(s) still open — count and end the drawer to close the day." Closing the day means the money is settled too, so BrewDesk won't let you sign off on a drawer nobody has counted.
The way out is right there. Next to that message is an "Open drawer" button — tap it, count the cash, tap END SESSION, and the Close Day button comes back to life. You never have to hunt through menus to get unstuck.
Someone went home without closing the drawer? The drawer screen shows "N cash drawer(s) were never closed" — "Someone left without ending the drawer. You cannot count it now, so closing it here records it as not counted — no number is invented." Tap "Close without counting" and you're free to carry on. The day is recorded honestly as "Cash wasn't counted" rather than as a tidy zero.
Count first — the numbers come after. Until you type what you counted, BrewDesk hides the expected total (and the cash-sales and cash-refund lines that add up to it). In their place it says "Shown after you enter what you counted" and, underneath, "Count the drawer first, then the numbers appear. This is so the count stays a count." Your opening float and your pay-ins and pay-outs stay visible the whole time — you did those yourself, so there's nothing to hide.
If the money doesn't match, one line is required. When the variance isn't zero, BrewDesk asks "Why is it different?" and won't let you close the drawer until something is written there (a blank space doesn't count). It nudges you with "Write one line before closing. In three days nobody will remember." — and it's right. If the variance is zero it doesn't ask at all.
Who the drawer belongs to is picked, not typed. The old free-text name box is gone. If you use staff accounts, choose the person from the list under "Drawer held by". If you don't use staff accounts, it simply says "Owner" — "Staff accounts are off, so this drawer is under the owner." The "Recorded by" box on a pay-in or pay-out is now read-only and follows the same person, so "who took that money out" only ever has one answer.
Money leaving the drawer for supplies? There's now a "Business expense" reason on Pay-Out, alongside Safe Drop, Tip Out, Bank Drop and Other. Give it its own reason and at the end of the month you can actually add up where the cash went.
Typed the opening float wrong? A "Fix opening float" button appears while nothing has happened yet — no cash sale, no pay-in, no pay-out. After the first cash sale it disappears, because by then changing the starting number would quietly move a real difference somewhere else. From that point on, write it in the variance reason at closing instead. The correction is added as a note (who, when, the old figure) — it never overwrites the original.
A red band you can't dismiss. If a drawer change doesn't reach the server, you'll see "This drawer has not been saved" — "The last change did not reach the server. If you refresh now, the float, pay-ins and count are gone. Check the network and make any change again." There is no close button on purpose. A message you can swipe away is a message you'll forget, and refreshing the page would lose the day's drawer.
